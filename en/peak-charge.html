How to lower a factory demand charge in Korea

One 15 minute peak can set a full year of demand charges.

Short answer

A factory demand charge is set by billing demand, not by contract demand. Billing demand is the largest 15 minute maximum demand among December, January, February, July, August and September of the previous 12 months including the meter reading month, and the current month. If it is below 30% of contract demand, 30% applies (Article 68 of the KEPCO Basic Supply Terms and Conditions (한전 기본공급약관)). So one short peak raises the bill for a year. Ways to lower it: 1) find peak dates and times from bills and 15 minute data, 2) stagger equipment starts that overlap in the same 15 minutes, 3) correct power factor with capacitors if it is under 90%, 4) review the selectable tariff options (I, II, III), 5) if peaks repeat every month, discharge an ESS at the peak. A short peak sets the whole year

  • At one factory in Chungnam, about 650 kW recorded over 15 minutes on a January morning became the billing demand for the next 12 months.
  • At the same factory, demand exceeded 600 kW for only 19 hours in a year (figures rounded).
  • At the industrial (B) high voltage A, option II demand rate of 8,320 KRW/kW, every 10 kW of peak removed cuts the demand charge by about KRW 1 million a year.

Ways to lower the peak

  • Stagger equipment starts so they do not overlap in the same 15 minutes.
  • Discharge an ESS at peak times to lower maximum demand.
  • Run deferrable loads such as EV charging in light load hours, 22:00 to 08:00. Use in these hours does not count toward billing demand.

Before you apply for more capacity

  • If new equipment runs heavily for only short periods, peak management can be better than raising contract demand.
  • Added contract demand normally carries a standard facility charge (Articles 84 and 93 of the KEPCO Basic Supply Terms and Conditions (한전 기본공급약관)).
  • The last 12 months of bills and 15 minute data show which way is right.
