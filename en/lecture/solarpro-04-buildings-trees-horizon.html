Solar Pro Lesson 4: Buildings, trees and horizon, with horizon azimuth starting at north 0°

Adding a building (corners clockwise), a tree (height and radius) and a horizon table in Solar Pro, the difference between horizon azimuth north 0° and array azimuth south 0°, and the overlap warning.

Lesson summary

The narration is in Korean with English subtitles (CC). The text below follows the narration. All examples are virtual plants.

Lesson 4 adds the three things that cast shade: buildings, trees and distant hills. The example is the virtual Mokpo ground mounted plant from lesson 2.

Building in the 3D CAD menu opens a window with position X −14 m, Y 10 m and height 6 m. The corners are entered clockwise in the table below: (0, 0), (0, 10), (8, 10), (8, 0) for a block 8 m wide and 10 m long. Refresh Image shows the shape, and OK puts the building to the left of the array with its shadow falling toward the array.

Next is Tree, at X 34 m, Y 8 m, with a tree height of 10 m and a trunk radius of 0.3 m. The table takes points of the tree's right side profile, (3, 3) and (3, 10), meaning foliage with a 3 m radius from 3 m up to 10 m. Leaf Fall is where each month is marked with or without leaves. OK puts the tree to the right of the array.

Distant hills go into the Horizon window as a table of azimuth and elevation. The yellow band below is the horizon, and where it overlaps the sun path the sun is blocked. The horizon is not shown in the 3D view but it is included in the calculation. One thing to watch: in the Solar Pro horizon table north is 0°, east 90° and south 180°. PVsyst horizon values use south as 0°, so add 180 when copying them across. The array azimuth entered in the lesson 2 wizard uses due south as 0°, so nothing is added to the array. The Direction field in the Building window is not an azimuth, it rotates the building.

Moving the building onto the array shows red text at the bottom left, Overlapping Objects with the names of the objects, and the calculation is blocked until the building is moved back. In short: buildings by corners entered clockwise, trees by radius and height, distant hills by the horizon table, and overlapping objects block the calculation. The scene is saved for lesson 5, where the shadows are viewed.

Frequently asked questions

Where is 0° in the Solar Pro horizon table?

North is 0°, east 90° and south 180°. PVsyst horizon values, where south is 0°, need 180 added when copied across. The array azimuth stays at due south 0°.

In what order do I enter building corners in Solar Pro?

Clockwise. In the lesson, (0, 0), (0, 10), (8, 10), (8, 0) made a building 8 m wide and 10 m long.

What do I do about the Overlapping Objects warning?

A building or tree overlaps the array and the calculation is blocked. Move the overlapping object and the warning disappears.
