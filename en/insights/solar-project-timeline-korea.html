Solar project timeline in Korea: how long each stage really takes, from license to commercial operation

Dated timelines from two factory roofs (1.35 MW through the power exchange in 9.5 months, 558 kW under a KEPCO agreement in 12 months), the statutory day counts, equipment lead times and what runs in parallel.

Permitting · ECO K KOREA engineering team · 2026-11-12

The short answer

On the factory roofs we have taken from license to commercial operation, the whole process took 9.5 months for a 1.35 MW plant selling through the power exchange and 12 months for a 558 kW plant under a KEPCO purchase agreement. Construction itself was two to six months of that. The rest was permits, the safety corporation review and utility paperwork, and only a few of those steps have a statutory clock: 60 days for the license, an inspection request at least 7 days before the inspection, a certificate within 5 days of it, and a commencement report within 30 days of the first power sale.

The seven stages and who handles each are in our note on Korean solar permitting. This note is about time: how long each stage took on two real projects, which stages can run in parallel, what the law fixes, what the suppliers fix, and where the months were lost.

Two tracks, decided by size

Above 1,000 kW the power is sold through the Korea Power Exchange, which adds membership, meter sealing by the exchange and a trading start confirmation. At 1,000 kW and below the owner signs a power purchase agreement with KEPCO, which adds the contract paperwork and a KEPCO protection coordination review before the meter is sealed. The permits before construction are the same on both tracks.

What ran in parallel, and what could not

  • The KEPCO application and, above 1,000 kW, the power exchange membership were filed three weeks after the license, while the structural and KESCO reviews were still open. They do not wait for the construction plan notification.
  • On the PPA track the purchase agreement documents went in before construction started; the agreement itself was signed only after the inspection certificate. Inverter setting confirmations, meter test certificates and other supplier documents for the agreement were requested at the time of ordering.
  • The extra high voltage switchgear and transformer take about 75 days from drawing approval to delivery in our schedules and must be ordered before the construction start date, which the notification itself permits because the notification concerns the work, not the purchase.
  • The structure notification cannot be accepted until the building set is final; in Cheonan it was accepted, cancelled and refiled when the buildings changed, and the license had to be amended for the same reason.
  • The pre use inspection cannot be requested until the notified plan and the built plant match, so every change of module, inverter or protection relay after the notification is filed as an amendment before the inspection request.

Where the months went

  • Reviews with supplements: seven KESCO supplements and four structure notification supplements in Changwon, five KESCO supplements in Cheonan. Almost all were mismatches between documents (module certificate numbers, relay models, string currents) and are avoidable with one cross check before filing.
  • Weather and strikes: six weeks of rain and a ready mix concrete strike in Changwon moved the completion from 30 June to 31 July and required two change notifications.
  • Roof decisions: four months in Cheonan between the license and a final building set, which then forced a license amendment and a second structure notification.
  • Forms: the building department asked for the structural confirmation on a form with seismic items, and the engineer had to rewrite and seal it.

What to settle before the clock starts

  • The final set of buildings and the capacity, because both appear on the license and on every later form.
  • The structural review and the roof material survey, before the structure notification.
  • The KESCO checklist items that produced supplements on our sites: working space layout, flood protection and fencing of outdoor equipment, switchgear sections, earthing details and string polarity marking.
  • Supplier documents for the KEPCO agreement or exchange registration, requested with the purchase order.
