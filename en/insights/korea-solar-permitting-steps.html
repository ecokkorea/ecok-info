Solar permitting in Korea: the seven steps, statutory periods and a real timeline

License, development permit, KEPCO connection, pre technical review, construction plan notification, pre use inspection and commencement report, with dates from a 1.35 MW roof.

Permitting · ECO K KOREA engineering team · 2026-11-12

The short answer

A commercial solar plant in Korea goes through seven stages: feasibility, the generation business license (60 day statutory period), the development permit, the KEPCO connection, the pre technical review and construction plan notification (before any work starts), the pre use inspection (applied for at least 7 days before the inspection date) and commercial operation with a business commencement report. Only some of the stages have a statutory period, so the whole process ran about ten months on a 1.35 MW factory roof we handled.

Foreign developers often ask for a single number: how long does permitting take in Korea? There is no single number, because the law fixes a period for some stages and leaves others to the local office or to KEPCO. What the law does fix is the order. Some steps cannot be swapped, and a plan that ignores that order loses months. Below is the sequence as written in the Electric Utility Act (전기사업법) and its enforcement rule, followed by the real dates from a project.

This article is about a plant that sells power. For a self consumption plant the first four stages are the same and the rest switches to the net metering or private use track, which we cover in a separate note on net metering rules.

Four steps that cannot be reordered

  • Construction plan notification before construction. Article 61(3) of the Electric Utility Act requires the notification before work starts. If the module, inverter or protection relay changes after the notification, an amendment is filed before the pre use inspection.
  • Pre technical review before the notification. A plant connected at extra high voltage, or of 500 kW or more, must attach the KESCO review report to its notification. Only PV under 500 kW connected at low voltage is exempt (Appended Table 8, Note 3 of the enforcement rule).
  • Supervisor assignment before construction. Every new PV plant needs construction supervision regardless of capacity; only general use installations of 10 kW or less are exempt. The assignment confirmation can be filed up to the start date.
  • Pre use inspection before commercial operation. The application goes in at least 7 days before the desired inspection date, and the meter is sealed and power is sold only after a pass.

What the notification packet looks like

The construction plan notification is a single form (Form 26 of the enforcement rule) with attachments in a fixed order: the construction plan, the schedule, a topographic map at 1:25,000, the technical specification, the supervisor assignment confirmation and the technical data. The construction plan itself lists the module type, rated output, open circuit voltage, short circuit current and module count, the inverter capacity and the protection devices (Appended Table 8, item 2). Two details trip up first time applicants: the start date on the schedule must fall after the notification date, and the pre use inspection application date must be at least 7 days before the inspection date.

Real dates from a 1.35 MW factory roof

These are the recorded dates from a 1,349.12 kWp roof at a manufacturing plant in the Changwon national industrial complex, connected at extra high voltage, for which we did the design, permitting and construction. Because it exceeds 1,000 kW, the power is sold through the Korea Power Exchange rather than directly to KEPCO.

1 August 2026Business commencement report filed

The longest stage was not one with a statutory period. The pre technical review and its supplements took about four months. On another factory roof of 558 kW we handled, the review asked for a working space layout, flood protection for outdoor equipment, a grounding terminal box detail and polarity marking on the module output wiring. Putting those four items into the first set of drawings is the single cheapest way to shorten a Korean permitting schedule.

The business commencement report must be filed within 30 days of the first power trade (Article 9 of the Act). The dated story of a comparable project, with the license, the notification and the inspection records, is in our note on solar permitting in Korea.

Where the offices differ

Whether the province has delegated the license and the notification to the city or county differs from province to province, and the offices in Jeollanam-do and Gwangju follow the rules of the integrated special city from 1 July 2026. We confirm the receiving office before the first filing rather than assuming it from a previous project.
