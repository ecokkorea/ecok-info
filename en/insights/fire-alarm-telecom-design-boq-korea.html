Fire alarm and telecom design and BOQ in Korea: separate contracts, detector counts and the quantities that get missed

Why fire and telecom work are contracted apart from electrical in Korea, the NFTC 203 detector area table with worked counts, the tray and interface boundaries, and the items that go missing.

BOQ · ECO K KOREA engineering team · 2026-11-12

The short answer

In Korea, fire alarm work and telecom work are contracted separately from electrical work by law, so a building produces three bills of quantities that share the same drawings but not the same boundaries. The fire alarm BOQ starts from a detector count fixed by the national fire safety standard (NFTC 203): floor area divided by the area one detector may cover, which is 70 m² for the common rate of rise detector under a fire resistant ceiling below 4 m and only 25 m² in a steel shed with a 4 to 8 m ceiling. The quantities that go missing are the interface wiring, the risers, the slack and the passive telecom parts.

Foreign contractors who win a Korean building project usually hold the electrical package and are surprised to learn that the fire alarm and the telecom packages are awarded to other licensed firms. The drawings still have to be coordinated, and the quantity take off is often done by the same office for all three. This note explains the legal split, where the boundaries run, how detector counts are fixed, and what we check before a fire or telecom BOQ goes out. The take off method for conductors and conduit is common to all three trades and is in our note on electrical BOQ workflow.

Three laws, three contracts

TradeLawRule
ElectricalElectrical Construction Business Act (전기공사업법), Article 11(1)Electrical work is to be awarded separately from work of other trades
Fire alarm and fire protectionFirefighting System Installation Business Act (소방시설공사업법), Article 21(2)Fire system work is to be contracted separately from other trades, with exceptions by presidential decree where separation is impractical
TelecomInformation and Communications Construction Business Act (정보통신공사업법), Article 25Telecom work is to be contracted separately from construction and electrical work, with exceptions by decree

The practical consequence is that the contract BOQ, the progress BOQs, the change order BOQs and the as built BOQ are all produced per trade, each with its own evidence sheets, and a quantity moved from one trade to another is a change order on both.

Where the boundary runs

  • Cable tray. A note on the fire alarm riser diagram such as 'cable tray used for fire systems belongs to the power package' puts the tray in the electrical BOQ and only the fire wiring inside it in the fire BOQ, and the conduit quantity is reduced by the tray length.
  • Interface wiring between the fire alarm receiver and the motor control centre, the public address system and exit lighting is claimed by whichever trade the drawing note and the contract BOQ assign it to; we check both before counting.
  • CCTV and access control equipment, with their power and signal cables, sit on the telecom side of the line in most Korean contracts, while the socket outlet that feeds the rack is electrical.
  • Fire pump and extinguishing system packages come with their own wiring tables (segment, number of conductors, size), and those tables are the quantity evidence for the fire BOQ.

Detector count: the five steps

The fire alarm design begins with a count, and the count is a formula: detectors equal floor area divided by the detection area of one detector, rounded up per room. Choosing the detection area takes five steps, all from the current standard.

Two worked counts show the range. A 20 by 15 m office of 300 m² with a reinforced concrete structure, a 3 m ceiling and class 2 rate of rise detectors needs 300 divided by 70, which is 4.29, so 5 detectors; the same office in a non fire resistant structure needs 8, and with class 2 smoke detectors only 2. A 40 by 30 m steel sandwich panel factory of 1,200 m² with a 6 m ceiling and class 2 rate of rise detectors needs 1,200 divided by 25, which is 48; switching to class 2 smoke detectors gives 1,200 divided by 75, which is 16. Smoke detectors stay at least 0.6 m from walls and beams, every detector at least 1.5 m from an air supply outlet, and a corridor gets one smoke detector per 30 m of walking distance. Whether a steel frame building counts as fire resistant is read from the building register and the structural drawings, not assumed. Our free detector calculator (Korean) applies these tables.

From the count to the fire BOQ

  • Count conductors per wiring tag, not per segment. The tags on the riser distinguish heat resistant wire (HIV) from fire resistant cable (FR-3) of the same size, and the two are different items with different prices. A segment carrying tags 18, 4, 10 and 11 is four quantities, each conductors times length.
  • Count devices on the floor plans by floor and reconcile them with the riser: detectors, manual call points, bells, indicator lamps and manual control boxes.
  • Add the vertical runs between floors and the drops from the ceiling to each device; these are the lines most often missing from a tender BOQ.
  • Take the extinguishing system wiring from the package wiring table and keep the table as the evidence sheet.
  • Size conduit from the conductor count in each segment and reduce it where cable tray carries the circuit.

What goes missing in the telecom BOQ

  • Slack at terminations and at the rack end of every cable run; the plan length alone is always short.
  • Passive parts that do not appear as symbols: racks, terminal boxes, patch panels, cable management.
  • The boundary for CCTV and access control, both the equipment and the power and signal cables feeding it.
  • Grounding of racks and shields, which is sometimes in the electrical BOQ and sometimes in the telecom BOQ depending on the drawing note.

Progress, change order and as built

For every trade the rules are the same: keep the items, specifications and units of the contract BOQ unchanged and vary only the quantities; show previous, current, cumulative and remaining columns; never let a cumulative quantity exceed the contract quantity, because the excess has to go through a change order; and keep an evidence sheet per trade with the drawing number, segment, wiring tag, conductor count and length behind every line. Public owners in Korea pay progress at least every 30 days under the national contract regulations, so the cycle is frequent and a BOQ that does not line up with the contract comes straight back.
