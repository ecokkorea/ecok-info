Rooftop solar structural review in Korea: loads, anchors, reinforcement and the permits that follow

KDS 41 wind and snow loads, roof zone pressures, rail and clamp pull out, the 7.5 kN anchor check on a 1.35 MW roof, new beams and columns under a major repair permit, and what KESCO asks for at inspection.

Structure · ECO K KOREA engineering team · 2026-11-12

The short answer

Before modules go on a Korean factory roof, a licensed structural engineer checks the new support structure and the existing building for five loads under the Korean building design load code (KDS 41 12 00): dead, live, snow, wind and seismic. The structure is notified to the building department before work starts, and if the building cannot carry the load it is reinforced under a major repair permit. On a 1.35 MW roof we handled, every anchor between roof and structure was checked for a pull out of 7.5 kN; on a 558 kW site, one building needed new beams and columns and the permit added about five weeks.

Foreign EPC contractors are used to a structural sign off, but the Korean sequence has two features that catch people out. The support structure is treated as a separate structure with its own notification under the Building Act (건축법 Article 83), and the Korea Electrical Safety Corporation asks for the structural calculation again at the pre use inspection. This note sets out what is checked, with the numbers from two of our roofs, and what to prepare.

Two structures, one engineer

The review covers the new support structure (rails, clamps and, for tilted arrays, the frames) and the existing building that receives the added weight and the wind uplift. Both are signed by a structural engineer licensed in Korea (건축구조기술사). Our own calculations prepare the model, the load cases and the member checks; the engineer reviews and seals. Where the building drawings are missing, a site survey of member sizes and spans replaces them, and that survey is the first thing to commission on an older plant.

The five loads and where the numbers come from

LoadWhat it is for a rooftop arrayCode basis
DeadModules, rails, clamps and ballast; typical modules weigh 22 to 35 kg eachMeasured weights from the datasheets

SeismicChecked on the building; the array itself is light and wind governs its membersKDS 41 17 00; the building check uses importance class and zone factor

Two inputs decide most of the result. The basic wind speed is read from a national map with a 500 year return period and differs more than people expect: about 28 m/s inland at Cheonan or Naju, 36 m/s at Changwon on the south coast, 42 m/s at Busan. The ground snow load map is being revised in 2026; typical values are 0.9 kN/m² around Cheonan, 0.6 at Changwon and 2.0 on the west coast at Yeonggwang, and the revised map must be checked when the calculation is sealed.

Wind on a low metal roof

For arrays laid parallel to a low roof (eave height under 20 m, slope up to 10 degrees) the code gives peak external pressure coefficients by roof zone: about −2.1 in the interior zone, −3.9 along the edges and −6.0 at the corners for small tributary areas, all suction. We apply these to the modules without taking credit for pressure equalisation under the module, which is conservative. Tilted arrays on a roof are treated as free standing monoslope roofs with the open flow coefficients, which is why a 15 degree tilt on a roof costs noticeably more structure than a flush installation. The design combinations are the strength design set of the code, with 1.2D + 1.6S + 0.5W, 1.2D + 1.0W + 0.5S and 0.9D + 1.0W among them; the last one, with reduced dead load and full wind, is the one that sizes the clamps.

Rails, clamps and the pull out number

On a trapezoidal or standing seam steel roof the rail runs across the ribs and is checked as a continuous beam supported at every rib, with a tributary width equal to the module short side divided by the number of rails. The reaction at each support under the uplift combination is the force that one clamp or one screw group has to resist, and it is compared with the manufacturer's test value for that clamp on that profile. The rib pitch, sheet thickness and purlin spacing therefore belong in the calculation input, not just the module count. On the 1.35 MW Changwon roof the fixing between the existing building and the new structure was specified to resist 7.5 kN of pull out at each point, the roof sheet was overlaid with new steel before installation, and the structure was a fixed aluminium alloy (6063-T5) system holding the modules at 15 degrees on a 6 degree roof.

When the building fails the check

At the 558 kW site in Cheonan one of six buildings, a single storey steel shed of 485 m², could not carry the added load. The engineer's first calculation was issued in May; when the set of buildings changed, a second calculation on 23 July assumed added beams and columns, and the application for a major repair permit under Article 11 of the Building Act went in on 29 July. The city's building department asked for the structural safety confirmation on a form that includes seismic items, so the engineer rewrote and sealed it: KDS 41, importance class 2, zone factor 0.22, base shear 34.2 kN, storey drift within limits. The permit and the structure notification were processed together on 1 September, the reinforcement was built in October, and the modules went on in December. The same site had a 1989 slate roof containing asbestos; drilling it cracks the sheets and standing on it is unsafe, so three options were priced: a steel overlay, removal and a new roof, or installation on insulation board with a waterproof membrane.

The notification and the inspection

  • Structure notification (Building Act Article 83) to the city or county before construction, with the structural calculation and drawings. It can be cancelled and refiled if the buildings change, as happened in Cheonan.
  • Major repair permit (Building Act Article 11) when beams, columns or the roof structure are added or replaced; processed with the structure notification.
  • The construction plan notification under the Electric Utility Act lists the support structure design and structural calculation among its technical documents.
  • At the pre use inspection, KESCO's inspection notice asks for the support structure design and structural calculation or a structural safety confirmation, and for rooftop plants also a waterproofing inspection record and a fall protection inspection record.
  • Special conditions written in the structural calculation, for example a snow clearing rule or a load limit on part of the roof, remain conditions for the life of the plant.

Our rule on 2D and 3D

For ground, carport and agrivoltaic structures we run a 2D frame and a 3D model and size each member on the larger demand, keeping every ratio and every drift at or below 95% of the limit. The rule came from a case where the 2D frame read the inner columns at half their real force. Roof rails are simpler, but the same 95% margin applies, and the clamp capacity is always taken from a test certificate, never from a catalogue number.

What to send for a quick first answer

  • Building register extract with the year of completion and the structure type.
  • Existing structural drawings, or permission for a site survey if there are none.
  • Roof material, sheet profile and thickness, and whether it contains asbestos.
  • Drone or roof photographs showing the ribs, the ridge and any rooftop equipment.
  • Module count per building, which we turn into the weight and area table the building department expects.

With these we say within a week which buildings pass as they are, which need reinforcement and roughly how much capacity each roof can hold. The permitting steps that follow are in the note on Korean solar permitting, and the structural approach for ground mounted arrays is in agrivoltaic structure design basics.
