Electrical BOQ and quantity take-off workflow

From conductor counts per conduit segment to conduit sizes, take-off sheets and a Korean public format BOQ, with a worked example on one floor.

BOQ · ECO K KOREA engineering team · 2026-11-11

Quantities come from counting, not from estimating

A Korean electrical bill of quantities is built from the drawings, segment by segment: conductors per conduit run, times length, then conduit size from the conductor count, then the standard estimating allowances, then unit prices. If one link is skipped, the BOQ and the drawings stop agreeing, and that is where claims start.

We prepare design, progress, change order and as built BOQs for electrical, fire alarm and telecom work, and we check tender quantities against drawings before bids. This article shows the workflow we use, with a worked example on one floor of lighting and receptacles.

The chain behind every quantity

Each number in the BOQ should be traceable back to a calculation. For a lighting system the chain is: lighting calculation, number of luminaires, layout, conductor count per segment, conduit size, quantity, BOQ line. The same chain exists for power and fire alarm. When a reviewer asks why a line says 212 luminaires or 3,650 m of wire, the answer must be a sheet, not a ratio.

Step 1: name the conductors

  • Line (L): from the panel to the switch.
  • Neutral (N): from the panel to the luminaire.
  • Switched return: from the switch back to the luminaire.
  • Travellers: the two conductors between three way and four way switches.
  • Protective earth (PE): with every receptacle, and with lighting where the legend shows it.

We read the drawing legend first, because the way conductor counts are marked (tick marks or numbers) varies between designers.

The count changes along a corridor as circuits leave the run. In our example floor, the first corridor segment carries the office line and neutral, two travellers and the corridor return, five conductors in all; after the office branch the count drops to four. Each drop to a wall switch adds the vertical length from the ceiling, 1.5 m in the example.

Conduit is counted once per segment, wire once per conductor. Mixing the two is the most common error we find when checking tender BOQs.

Step 4: conduit size from the count

Conduit size follows the number and size of conductors. Our company conduit sizing sheet for 2.5 mm² PVC insulated wire in rigid PVC conduit gives 16 mm for up to three conductors and 22 mm for four or five. In the example, the 9.5 m of four and five conductor runs become 22 mm conduit, the remaining 31.5 m of lighting runs and the 21.5 m of receptacle runs become 16 mm.

Step 5: take-off sheet to BOQ

The take-off sheet lists each segment with length and conductor count, then applies the allowances of Korea's standard construction estimating rules before the quantities move to the BOQ. The BOQ itself is arranged the way Korean public owners expect: cost summary, trade summaries, trade BOQ with item, specification, unit and quantity, unit cost build ups and unit price analysis. Every line carries the same name and specification as the drawing legend, so a reviewer can search one in the other.

Fire alarm follows the same logic

For automatic fire detection the starting point is the detector count: floor area divided by the detection area of one detector, rounded up. The detection area depends on the structure and mounting height in the national fire safety standard (NFTC 203). A 20 by 15 m office of 300 m² with a fire resistant structure, ceilings under 4 m and rate of rise spot detectors of class 2 has a detection area of 70 m², so 300 divided by 70 gives 4.29, rounded up to 5 detectors. The same room with a non fire resistant structure needs 8. Then come wiring counts, conduit lengths and the number of receiver circuits, measured from the plan exactly as above.

What we check on a tender BOQ

  • Wire counted per conductor and conduit per segment, not mixed.
  • Conduit sizes consistent with the conductor count in each segment.
  • Vertical drops to switches, receptacles and panels included.
  • Allowances applied once, at the take-off stage, not again in the BOQ.
  • Item names and specifications identical to the drawing legend.

Working with us from abroad

Send the drawings in PDF or CAD and, if you have one, the contract or tender BOQ. We return the take-off sheets in Excel and the BOQ in the Korean public format, with an English item list on request. We work on projects anywhere in Korea and set the schedule after seeing the documents.
