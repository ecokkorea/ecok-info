Solar net metering savings calculator (Korea)

How much a solar system lowers a Korean electricity bill for a home, shop or factory, and how long it takes to pay back.

What the net metering calculator tells you

Net metering lets a customer with solar send the unused surplus to KEPCO and have it subtracted from the electricity received that month (Article 18 of the Korean guideline on trading small scale renewable power, solar up to 1,000 kW). Credit left over is carried to the next month.

The calculator compares solar output and load for each of the 8,760 hours, counts the smaller value as self consumption and the excess as surplus exported to KEPCO. For homes it includes the drop to a lower progressive tier after netting; for shops and factories it applies the seasonal energy rates and the contract demand charge as billed.

The right size is one whose yearly generation is close to or a little below yearly use. A large credit left over at year end means the system is too big. Change the capacity and look for the shortest payback.

Frequently asked questions

What is net metering?

You install solar where you use electricity and send the unused surplus to KEPCO. That surplus is subtracted from the electricity you received from KEPCO that month (Article 18 of the guideline on trading small scale renewable power). Generated electricity is valued at the same rate as the electricity you buy, and the two are netted on the meter reading date. Solar up to 1,000 kW qualifies.

What happens when generation exceeds use?

The remaining credit is subtracted from next month's supply (carried over). You can instead choose to be paid, but that rate is based on the weighted average system marginal price (SMP) for the period, which is often lower than the rate you pay. This calculator uses the carry over method and shows the credit left over at year end separately.

Why is the progressive tier effect so large for homes?

The energy charge is calculated on the energy after netting. A home that used to exceed 450 kWh (July and August) or 400 kWh (other months) and drops to 150 kWh after netting moves from the tier 3 rate (307.3 KRW) to the tier 1 rate (120.0 KRW). In the KEPCO example the demand charge tier follows the energy received before netting, but the page marks that rule as pending, so ⑧ lets you see both.
